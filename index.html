The first 48 hours

Grief is hard enough without a scavenger hunt.

When someone is suddenly in hospital, or gone, the family is left asking the same questions. Usually the only person who knew the answers is the one they can't ask.

"Is there a will? Where is it?"

Section 6 records where the will, power of attorney, and healthcare directive are kept. Just the location, not what they say.

"Which bank? Was there life insurance?"

Section 7 lists the institutions and policies that exist, so nothing gets missed. No balances, no account numbers.

"Who do we even call first?"

Sections 2 and 4 give a calm order of operations and the people to notify, starting with pets and dependents.

Fill it in once, keep it current, and tell one person where it lives. That single step turns a week of guessing into a list someone can follow.

Short on time? Do five things.

Emergency contacts and medications. Where the will is. Where financial information lives. Who to call. And telling one person the guide exists. About fifteen minutes, and the most useful information is already written down.

Designed to be safe to keep in a drawer. The guide never asks for passwords, PINs, Social Security numbers, or full account numbers. It records where that information is kept, not the information itself.

Questions

Before you buy

Is this a will?
No. It's a logistics guide: where things are, who to call, and what you'd want. It doesn't replace a will or advice from an estate planning lawyer, and it works alongside one. In fact, one of its main jobs is making sure your family can find the will.
What format is it? Can I fill it in on my computer?
Everything comes as a PDF. The main guide is fillable on screen and also prints cleanly, so you can type it or write it by hand. The label pack is designed for full-sheet sticker paper, printed at 100% size.
Is it safe to write all this down?
The guide is built so you never write passwords, PINs, Social Security numbers, or full account numbers in it. Instead you record where that information is stored, for example "in the password manager; the master password is with our attorney."
I'm buying this for my parents. Which should I get?
The bundle. Starting the Conversation is written for exactly this situation, with openers and replies for the most common pushback. A lot of people fill out their own copy alongside their parent's, which takes the pressure off.
How long does it take to fill out?
The five "Start Here" items take about fifteen minutes. The whole guide is meant to be done a section at a time, over weeks if you like. There's an update log at the end, and a reminder to review it every six months.
Can I print more than one copy?
Yes. Print a copy for yourself, one for each parent, and reprint whenever you update it.
