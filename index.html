The problem

When the time comes, how will your family know where everything is?

When a parent dies or can no longer speak for themselves, the family is suddenly in charge of everything. The one person who knew where it all was can't tell them anymore. So they search through drawers, guess at passwords, and argue about what Mom or Dad would have wanted, in the middle of the hardest weeks of their lives.

  • Which accounts exist?

    A savings account nobody knew about. A life insurance policy. A subscription still charging a card every month. Without a list, you find out by accident, if you find out at all.

  • Where are the important documents?

    You know there's a will, a deed, a power of attorney. You just don't know which drawer, which box, or which bank. So you keep looking.

  • Who should we call?

    The doctor, the attorney, the insurance agent, the people who should hear it from family first. You're making calls in the worst week of your life without knowing who is on the list.

  • What would they have wanted?

    Their care, their service, their final wishes. When nothing is written down, the family is left guessing, and sometimes fighting.

Your family deserves certainty, not guesswork. One conversation today and one organized guide mean they will know exactly where to look, who to call, and what you wanted.
